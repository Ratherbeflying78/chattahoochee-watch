Supporting context — live sensors upstream

The same samples as tables

Riverkeeper's Neighborhood Water Watch volunteers collect samples every week and culture them in a lab. These are measured counts, not the modelled estimates below — the strongest bacteria data available for this river. Their summer Swim Guide applies the same tests to popular access points.

    River cameras USGS

    These are the real thing: cameras mounted at USGS streamgages and pointed directly at the water, from the USGS National Imagery Management System. Each frame is stamped with the gauge number and time. Cameras shoot every 15 minutes during daylight, so expect dark frames overnight and a timestamp that lags the current moment.

    Bridge & roadway views

    Public GDOT / 511GA traffic cameras positioned at or near Chattahoochee River crossings, ordered downstream from Atlanta to West Point. Images refresh every 60 seconds. These are roadway cameras, not scenic river cams — and GDOT rotates capacity, so any individual camera may show an “image unavailable” placeholder. Use them to eyeball conditions, not to judge river stage.

    Notes and gaps

    • There is a USGS camera at Morgan Falls Dam — gauge 02335815, one of the gauges on the River Profile map — but it has been dormant since March 2025 and USGS currently flags it as hidden, so it is not shown here. If it comes back online it will appear automatically.
    • No public dam cameras. Neither the Corps of Engineers at West Point Dam nor Buford Dam publishes an embeddable live camera feed.
    • No National Park Service cams. The Chattahoochee River National Recreation Area does not run a public webcam.
    • Video streams are locked. GDOT does publish HLS video for its cameras, but the streams require authentication, so only the still images can be shown here.
    • Daylight only. USGS river cameras shoot during daylight hours, so overnight the most recent frame will be dark and its timestamp will drift until shooting resumes after sunrise.
